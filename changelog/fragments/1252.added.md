- `ScipyGlobalOpt` (the SciPy global optimization library) gains the `BASINHOPPING` algorithm, wrapping
  `scipy.optimize.basinhopping`. Its local minimization method is chosen with `local_method`, which is by default
  SLSQP for a problem with constraints and L-BFGS-B otherwise when the objective and the constraints have gradients
  or when the `differentiation_method` approximates them (finite differences or complex step), and, when they do not,
  COBYLA for a problem with inequality constraints only, COBYQA for a problem with equality constraints and
  Nelder-Mead for a problem without constraints; problems with constraints require `SLSQP`,
  `COBYLA` (inequality constraints only), `COBYQA` or `trust-constr`, and `L-BFGS-B`, `TNC`, `SLSQP` and
  `trust-constr` use the gradient of the objective. The random displacements stay within the bounds of the design
  space. Its settings are `BASINHOPPING_Settings`, and the values of `local_method` are those of the
  `BasinHoppingLocalMethod` enumeration, available from `gemseo.enum`. `niter_success` stops the run when the best
  local minimum no longer improves, and `accept_test` takes a user test to accept or reject a local minimum.
- `ScipyGlobalOpt` gains the `DIRECT` algorithm, wrapping `scipy.optimize.direct`. It uses the locally biased variant
  DIRECT-L by default, controlled by `locally_biased`, and requires finite bounds. Its `max_iter` is 1,000 by default
  and at most 10,000,000, as SciPy's DIRECT allocates memory in proportion to it. Its settings are
  `DIRECT_Settings`.
- `OptimizationAlgorithmDescription.handle_unbounded_variables` tells whether an optimization algorithm handles variables
  with infinite bounds; an algorithm that does not is considered unsuited to such problems.
